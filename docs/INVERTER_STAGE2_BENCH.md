# Inverter: stage 2 (injection test) and stage 3 (closed loop), firmware v0.8.0

**The inverter has never run on live IGBTs in this firmware.** The PC tests prove the logic (sequence, ramp, verdict, fault order); they cannot prove the hardware. Every step below is a gate: do not go on until the one before it looks right.

## What the bench has shown so far
* **Gate 2, input about 110 V, 2 %:** `TF OK`, +0.7 V. **Gate 3, input 200 to 250 V, 2 %:** `TF OK`, **+1.9 to +2.1 V**, in the expected direction for both boost (200 V) and buck (250 V). The transformer, the leg selection and the dead-time handling are right.
* **The injected change at 2 % (64 ticks):** 0.7 V at about 110 V, 1.9 V at 200 V, 2.1 V at 250 V. **At 5 % (160 ticks), v0.8.0:** **+2.8 V at 200 V and +3.4 V at 250 V.** **With the wider file (cap 1600 ticks):** about 20 V of boost and buck. Buck and boost are nearly equal. (An earlier version of this document extrapolated the 2 % gain, about 0.03 V per tick, to large duty. That was WRONG: see the curve below.)
* **The measured curve (no load).** It is STEEP over the first 64 ticks (about 1.9 to 2.1 V) and then flattens to about **0.009 V per tick at 200 V and 0.0135 V per tick at 250 V** (proportional to Vin): the transformer plus this drive delivers only about **15 to 17 % of Vin per unit duty** (the old firmware's regulator constant `0.9/5.78 = 0.156` is a close match). Corrections, effective ticks -> volts: 64 -> 1.9 / 2.1, 160 -> 2.8 / 3.4, 1600 -> about 17 / 22, 2752 (the 90 % duty ceiling) -> about 27 / 38 (extrapolated), at 200 V / 250 V. A plausible cause of the steep start is that the IGBT conducts about 1 to 2 us longer than commanded (turn-off delay and the dead time acting as extra on-time); that is a hypothesis.
* **What the curve allows** (the regulator needs the correction to reach 230 V): cap **640 ticks** = about 7.5 V at 200 V and 9.6 V at 250 V, so it reaches 230 V only from about **222 to 239 V**; cap **1600** = 17 / 22 V, **212 to 252 V**; cap **2000** = 20.5 / 27.6 V, **208 to 259 V**; cap **2752** (the ceiling) = 27 / 38 V, **202 to 270 V** (the upper figure extrapolated above 250 V). **So this transformer with this drive regulates a window of about 202 to 270 V, roughly +13 % / -15 %, not the old firmware's 180 to 280 V (+28 % / -18 %).** Either the production transformer has a larger ratio than the test pieces, or the product range has to be re-specified. Outside the window the unit saturates at the cap: it corrects as much as it can (for example 195 V in gives about 222 V out). ALL OF THIS IS NO-LOAD: with a load the dead time acts as a voltage error in the direction of the current, and that has not been measured.
* **The first closed-loop report ("8 to 10 V") was the cap, not demand:** a 640-tick cap gives only 7.5 to 9.6 V, so the output was saturating, not regulating inside its range. The wider file (cap 1600) then gave about 20 V, as the curve predicts.

## Stage 3, the closed loop (v0.8.0)
* **The regulator** is the old firmware's integral controller (P and D removed; a test proves it bit-identical to the old code with those terms at zero): the buck / boost mode is decided by the INPUT against a RAMPED setpoint, the setpoint starts at the present input and slides to 230 V (3 V/s for 2 s, then 10 V/s, the last 3 V at 5 V/s: bumpless), and there is a freeze band of +/-0.75 V in which no correction is applied.
* **New in v0.8.0:** the regulator's output is the EFFECTIVE high-side on-time and the timer CCR is dead time + that (zero stays zero: a pulse at or below the 128-tick dead time outputs nothing); a **cap** (`CTRL_STAGE3_MAX_EFF_TICKS`) on the effective on-time that also clamps the integrator (no wind-up); the stage-3 legacy relay path (same unwinding as stage 2).
* **Study against YOUR plant** (the real regulator module, your measured gain curve, the dead time, a 20 ms RMS delay, noise, duty updated once per half-cycle; now a permanent test): at the measured gain, at half and at double it: constant inputs settle within 0.2 V with about 5 ticks of duty wobble; 10 V/s variac ramps across the 230 V crossover are followed within 1.2 V; an INSTANTANEOUS 30 to 40 V input step is recovered in 0.1 to 0.2 s. **The buck loop is the sensitive side:** the old firmware scheduled its buck gain 1.54x higher than the boost gain for the OLD transformer, but your buck and boost gains are equal, so it is about 1.7x more aggressive than designed. It still has about 2x margin; at 3x the measured gain it oscillates. 3x would be 0.1 V per tick, the "tuned up to about 0.1 V/count" limit stated in the regulator header, and the physical ceiling is near the measured value at 250 V, so the margin is real, but it is a simulation.
* **What is NOT known:** the plant gain at LARGE duty (only 2 % pulses were measured), and how the real RMS measurement, the relay and the line behave in a closed loop. That is what the first run is for.

## Gate 4: the first closed-loop run, NO LOAD
Config **`slave_config_STAGE3_first_run_cap640.h`**: stage 3, starts only between 200 and 250 V (trips outside 190 to 260 V), correction capped at about 8 to 10 V (cap 640 ticks: it reaches 230 V only from about 222 to 239 V, saturating outside that is EXPECTED), **trips if the output exceeds 250 V**. Output load disconnected, series lamp in. Set the variac to about **215 to 225 V before power-up**.
What you should see: the same start as before up to `RUN F00`; then Vout starts at Vin and slides towards 230 V (from 215 V in about 4 s) and settles at **230 V +/- 1.5 V** while Vin does not change; the lamp does not brighten; no fault.
Then, each step only if the one before looks right: (1) hold two minutes, note any wobble beyond +/-1 V; (2) turn the variac slowly (a few volts per second) between 205 and 245 V: the output should stay at 230 V +/- 1.5 V, with no hunting as the input crosses 230 V; (3) hold 245 V (buck); (4) then widen the cap step by step, watching the transformer (heat, buzzing) and the lamp: `slave_config_STAGE3_wider_cap1600.h` (reaches 230 V from about 212 to 252 V), `slave_config_STAGE3_cap2000.h` (208 to 259 V), `slave_config_STAGE3_cap2752_full_range.h` (202 to 270 V, the duty ceiling).
**Row 3 now shows the regulator (v0.8.1, needs the MASTER update too):** every other 2 s it alternates with the normal row: `BOOST 464t` or `BUCK 312t` = the regulator's EFFECTIVE on-time in timer ticks (50 ticks = 0.78 us; the cap is 640 in the first-run file), `FREEZE` = inside the +/-0.75 V band, no correction at all, and a trailing `CAP` = the correction sits at the cap, so the output may not reach 230 V. If you never see these lines the master is still the old one.
**It also lets you check the curve:** at 5 or 6 input voltages write down Vin, Vout, the ticks and BOOST or BUCK once the output has settled; the correction |Vout - Vin| against the ticks should follow the measured curve above (for example about 17 V at 1600 ticks at 200 V). The ticks near 230 V input, where the corrections are 1 to 3 V, are the interesting part: the study says the loop does not hunt there, but that rests on an unmeasured first 64 ticks.
The cap means the output can only reach 230 V inside the window of the cap table above; outside it the output saturates at Vin plus the capped correction. That is expected, not a fault.
**Switch off at once** if Vout goes above about 240 V, the output swings by more than +/-3 V, the lamp brightens, or a fault appears. Send me: Vin and Vout at 205 / 215 / 230 / 245 V, the steady wobble, any fault code with its `dt=` line.

## Start-up time: why it takes a few seconds, and the fast-start option (v0.8.3)
**Bench:** switching on at 205 or 210 V, the output reaches 230 V "after a few seconds"; the correction looks very slow. That is the regulator's SOFT START, taken unchanged from the old firmware: the setpoint starts at the present input and slides to 230 V at **3 V/s for the first 2 s, then 10 V/s until within 3 V of the target, then 5 V/s**. The real regulator on the measured plant: **4.1 s from 205 V, 3.6 s from 210 V, 2.5 s from 220 V** (buck: 3.0 s from 245 V, 4.5 s from 260 V), counted from the start of RUN.
The whole switch-on is longer than that: about 2 s of `WAIT: R` (the relays), then **3 s with the converter alone** (`CTRL_RECT_TO_INV_MS`, the old firmware's rectifier-to-inverter delay), then the ramp: roughly 9 s from power-up to 230 V at 205 V. Once running, the loop itself is fast (an input step is recovered in 0.1 to 0.2 s in the study): the slowness is only the start.
**Option:** `slave_config_STAGE3_cap2752_full_range_fast_start.h` ramps at **10 V/s for 0.5 s, then 30 V/s, then 10 V/s** for the last 3 V: **1.4 s from 205 V, 1.2 s from 210 V, 0.9 s from 220 V** (no overshoot, no hunting in the study; `CTRL_SOFTSTART_*` settings, the old values stay the default). Caveats: the old values were chosen for a LOADED unit, and a faster ramp is a faster rise of the load voltage; this is no-load only. The 3 s converter-alone delay is NOT changed (I do not know why the old firmware has it, so I left it).
Tests: the ramp against an independent model (old and fast settings, upward and downward), and that a config setting really reaches the running regulator (each of the four settings, and the regulator itself, checked by breaking them one at a time).

## 0. Read first (safety)
* **Output load DISCONNECTED** (a meter on the output terminals is fine). **Series lamp in the mains line.** A lamp that suddenly glows brightly = switch off.
* **The board is mains-referenced.** Never clip a grounded scope probe or ground lead onto it. Either run the whole unit from an **isolation transformer**, or use **differential / isolated probes**. If in doubt, watch only the master display and the LEDs.
* Keep a hand on the variac / switch. After switching off, wait for the DC link to discharge before touching anything.
* **The output contactor.** v0.8.0 drives the contactor pins like the old firmware's start-up (CONT1 = PC0 HIGH = OFF); v0.7.x left them UNCONFIGURED, so the output triac stage was in whatever state the board's resistors gave it. Contactor CONTROL (closing it for a load) is still not implemented, and I do not know what CONT2 drives: **treat the output terminals as possibly live and measure them with a meter before touching them.**
* The first run is at reduced input (about 120 V) and the smallest effective duty (2 %), on purpose.

## What the first bench run showed, and the fix (v0.7.1)
v0.7.0 at "1 %" and input 100 to 120 V gave **`TF ? +0.0V`**: no effect at all. The cause was mine: the dead time is 2.0 us = **128 timer ticks**, and the 1 % test duty was 32 ticks (0.5 us). The timer does not generate a high-side pulse that is shorter than the dead time (RM0444), so at 1 %, 2 % and 3 % (32 / 64 / 96 ticks) **nothing was output**; the simulation did not model it. v0.7.1 makes `CTRL_STAGE2_DUTY_COUNTS` the EFFECTIVE on-time and adds the dead time on top: the timer CCR is 128 + the setting, so the real high-side pulse is exactly the setting. The test ladder is now **2 % (1.0 us), 3 % (1.5 us), 5 % (2.5 us)**. A test now checks that no command at or below the dead time is ever applied, and the simulation models the vanishing pulse (the exact v0.7.0 bug is caught: 19 failing checks).
**If `TF ? +0.0V` appears again at 5 %, the inverter is still not driving: look at the gate pulses on the scope (below) before anything else.** A result of exactly `0.0` means no measurable effect, not a failed measurement: the RMS result updates every 10 ms, so the baseline gets about 150 samples.

## What changed in the firmware (v0.7.0)
* **Stage 2 now works with the legacy relay sequence** (`SLAVE_RELAYS_MODE 2`), the one that works on your bench: 1L/2L on, SH pulse, W on, converter (positive half first, first diagonal at the line peak), and **3 s later the inverter**. The relays do not change: W stays on, SH off (the PRI nets are open, the inverter drives the primary).
* **Dead time 2.0 us** (it was 1.0 us): the old firmware runs 2.0 us (`TIM1_TIM15_UpdateParameters(0, 128)`), proven on these IGBTs. **Break filter 4** as the old firmware.
* Leg selection checked against the old code: boost duty goes to TIM15 on the positive half and TIM1 on the negative half, the same as the old firmware, and the PWM period is the same (3200 counts, 20 kHz).
* **Stage 2 is a one-shot injection test**, not a regulator: zero duty baseline 1.5 s, ramp 2 s (the effective on-time 0 -> `CTRL_STAGE2_DUTY_COUNTS`, default 64 ticks = 1.0 us = 2 %), hold 5 s (the last 3.5 s averaged), ramp down 1 s, then zero duty until you stop. The result is the CHANGE of (Vout - Vin) against the baseline, counted positive in the EXPECTED direction (boost: Vout up when Vin is below 230 V; buck: Vout down above 230 V). It appears on the master, row 3 every other 2 s: `TF OK +1.2V`, `TF FLIP -1.1V` or `TF ? +0.1V` (inside the +/-0.3 V dead band). If the change ever exceeds 15 V the duty goes to zero at once (`TF ?` with a large value).
* A **soft fault or a stop with the inverter running** unwinds in a controlled way: duty to zero first (the low sides keep the primary shorted), then both relay coils released, the inverter disabled only after the W contact has had time to close. An overcurrent-class fault (FLT / break) switches everything off at once, as the old firmware does. Stage 3 (closed loop) is still refused for the legacy relays: it comes after this test.

## 1. Gate 1: the dead time on the scope, NO MAINS
Config `slave_config_SCOPE_deadtime_check_NO_MAINS.h` (stage 0, 50 % debug PWM at 20 kHz on both legs). **Gate-driver supply only, no mains, no DC bus.** Probe a complementary pair of leg 1 (PB14 = 1H, PB13 = 1L) and of leg 2 (PA8 = 2H, PA7 = 2L), at the MCU pins or the gate-driver inputs.
Expect 20 kHz, 50 %, and **2.0 us (1.8 to 2.2) with both signals low** at each edge, never overlapping. If you see 1.0 us the old firmware is flashed. Please send a screenshot of one pair.

## 2. Gate 2: stage 2 at about 120 V, 2 %
Config `slave_config_STAGE2_inverter_120V_2pct.h` (start window 90 V). Set the variac to about 120 V, output load disconnected, lamp in. What you should see, in order:
1. `SLAVE R START` + `WAIT: R` (about 2 s): **1L and 2L LEDs on**, SH click, W click about 1 s later.
2. The converter starts (normal row 3 `50.0Hz L0 B0 f..`), the master shows Vin and Vout.
3. About 3 s later the inverter starts and the master shows `RUN F00`. For 1.5 s nothing changes (baseline), then 2 s ramp, 5 s hold (1H / 2H LEDs glow dimly, alternating with the half-cycles), 1 s ramp down: about 10 s in all.
   **On the scope (isolated / differential probe only), during the hold:** at the MCU pin of the HIGH side of the active leg (boost: 1H = PB14 in the positive half-cycle, 2H = PA8 in the negative half-cycle) you should see **a 20 kHz train of pulses 1.0 us wide** (2 %), the corresponding low side off while it is high, and 2.0 us with both low before the pulse. The other leg's high side stays low. No pulses = the inverter is not driving (this is what v0.7.0 did).
4. Row 3 then shows the result every other 2 s. The unit stays in RUN at zero duty; switch off when done.
What the result means:
* `TF OK +x.xV`: the transformer and the leg selection are right. **Write down x** (it is volts of injection at 1 % duty at this input voltage).
* `TF ? +0.1V`: too small to judge: use the 3 % file, then 5 %. (`TF ? +0.0V` exactly: see above, check the gate pulses first.)
* `TF FLIP -x.xV`: **stop.** The transformer (or a leg) acts the wrong way round. Power off, wait for the DC link to discharge, swap the two leads of ONE winding (not both), repeat. Do not go on until it reads OK.
* A fault (F01 / F02 with a `dt=` line, or others): note WHEN it happened (during the ramp? at what duty?) and the `dt=` line, switch off and tell me.
* Vout moving by more than a few volts, the lamp glowing, a smell, or any unusual noise: switch off.
